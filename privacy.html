§ 1

Definitions and Interpretive Framework

The following terms shall have the meanings set forth below. They are presented in plain English because The Operator writes AI evaluation rubrics for a living and has therefore lost the ability to tolerate unnecessary complexity. If context makes a definition obviously wrong, use context. You are an adult. You can figure it out.

§ 1.1 — "The Site"

The Site means jereme.xyz. It is a personal website. It has never been, is not currently, and will not become: a SaaS product, a social network, an e-commerce platform, a DAO, a community, or any other category of internet property that has a dedicated section in a venture capital pitch deck. It is a website. About a person. This definition will not expand.

§ 1.2 — "The Operator"

The Operator is Jereme Abernathy — independent AI practitioner, product designer, coffee person, and the individual responsible for every pixel on this site including the ones that aren't quite right on mobile yet. He does not have a data team. He has a laptop, a standing desk he keeps meaning to adjust, and an inbox that he actually reads.

§ 1.3 — "You" / "The User"

You means any person, company, government entity, automated system, language model, or inexplicably well-funded bot that has accessed The Site for any purpose. If you are a crawler trained to extract personal data from privacy policies specifically: hello. The irony is noted. The data you're looking for is in § 3 and it is going to be a disappointing read.

§ 1.4 — "Personal Data"

Personal Data means information that could identify You as a specific individual, as opposed to, say, a category of person, a demographic, or an abstract concept. On this site, Personal Data is limited almost entirely to whatever you voluntarily type and submit. The Site is not in the business of inferring your identity from your typing speed or the font size you set. That is a different kind of website. This is not that.

§ 1.5 — "The Ventures"

The Ventures means fw/r, pine, nrthrn, and drift — four independently operated commercial entities that are linked from this site because The Operator built them and this is his personal site. They appear here as hyperlinks. Hyperlinks are not mergers. The Ventures maintain separate governance, separate infrastructure, and separate legal exposure from this document. See § 8, which exists specifically because this distinction keeps needing to be made.

A brief note on reading legal definitions: you do not have to enjoy it. No one does. The Operator has written approximately eight hundred pages of AI evaluation rubrics and still finds defined terms sections to be a genre that could stand to evolve. Alas.

§ 2

What This Document Actually Is

§ 2.1 — The Reason This Exists

This Privacy Policy exists because at some point the internet decided that every website — regardless of whether it collects a single byte of personal data from a single user — must publish a Privacy Policy or be treated as legally suspect. The Operator has complied. You are reading the result. It took longer to write than the actual data infrastructure it describes, which should tell you something about where we are as a civilization.

§ 2.2 — What It Is Not

This is not a GDPR Article 30 record of processing activities. It is not a data processing agreement. It is not a BAA. It is not a DPIA. It is not applicable to any commercial transaction because there are no commercial transactions here. If you are looking for a privacy policy that governs an actual product with actual users who are actually generating meaningful data: this is the wrong URL and also probably the wrong industry for whatever you're building.

§ 2.3 — Scope of This Policy

This policy covers data handling on jereme.xyz only. It does not govern The Ventures (see § 8). It does not govern social media platforms linked from this site (see § 5.3). It does not govern your own browser, your ISP, your employer's network, or whatever other parties are observing your internet traffic for reasons that have nothing to do with The Operator and everything to do with the general surveillance infrastructure of the modern internet, which is a separate and much longer document.

Executive summary of this entire policy: it's a personal website. He doesn't sell data. He doesn't have a data team. He has Google Analytics and a contact form. That is the entire data operation. You may now close this tab.

§ 3

Information Collected

The following is an exhaustive list of information collected through use of The Site. It is presented with the same thoroughness applied to a much more invasive operation, which is a deliberate choice intended to demonstrate that thoroughness and invasiveness are not the same thing.

§ 3.1 — Contact Form Submissions

If You fill out and submit the contact form on The Site — labeled "signal" because "contact form" felt too 2009 — The Operator receives your name, your email address, and the content of your message. This is transmitted via Resend to The Operator's inbox and a Slack channel so he notices it. This information is used exclusively to respond to you. It is not sold, aggregated, added to a list, used to build a profile, or fed into any pipeline. The Operator will simply read it and write back. Like a person.

§ 3.2 — Google Analytics

The Site uses Google Analytics (GA4) to collect anonymous, aggregate traffic data. This means The Operator can see things like "seventy people visited this week, most of them from the US, many of them on iPhones, and one of them spent forty-five minutes on the about page." He cannot see who those people are. He cannot see their names. He genuinely cannot tell if his mom is included in that number, though statistically she probably is at least once a week.

§ 3.3 — Cloudflare Edge Logs

The Site runs through Cloudflare. Cloudflare logs standard server request data — IP addresses, request timestamps, user agents — as part of its normal and completely reasonable infrastructure operation. This is governed by Cloudflare's own privacy documentation, not this one. The Operator has access to aggregate Cloudflare analytics dashboards. He does not review individual request logs because doing so would be both invasive and extraordinarily boring.

§ 3.4 — Theme Preference

If You switch between dark and light mode, The Site stores your preference in your browser's local storage. This data lives in your browser. It does not leave your device. It does not go anywhere. It exists so the site does not reset to dark mode every time you open a new tab, which would be annoying and also kind of rude. This is the least interesting item in this entire document.

§ 4

Information Not Collected

For the avoidance of doubt, and because people find comfort in explicit lists of things that are not happening to them, the following is a comprehensive account of data The Site does not collect. Some of these items are obvious. They are included anyway because obvious things being obvious has never stopped anyone from asking about them.

  • Passwords — there is no login system. There is no account. There is nothing requiring authentication. If you are trying to log in to jereme.xyz, something has gone quite wrong and it is not something The Operator did.
  • Payment information — there is nothing to pay for. If someone is asking for your credit card on this website, you are being scammed and it is not by The Operator.
  • Biometric data — no cameras, no fingerprint readers, no voice analysis, no facial recognition, no gait detection. The website cannot see you. This should be reassuring.
  • Health or medical information — not applicable. The Operator is not a healthcare provider. If you are using a personal website to manage health information, this is the gentlest possible suggestion to reconsider your system.
  • Social Security numbers, government IDs, or financial account numbers — what are you doing here. Seriously. Go somewhere else with those.
  • Your precise geolocation — The Site knows approximately what country you are in, in the same way that a postcard knows where it was sent from. That is the end of its geographic ambition.
  • Your browsing history outside this domain — The Operator is not interested and also does not have the infrastructure to collect it even if he were, which he is not.
  • Your microphone, camera, contacts, photos, or any other device capability — the website is not an app. It cannot request permissions. It is a website.
  • Your startup idea — you should be more protective of that elsewhere, honestly. The internet is full of people who would like it.
  • Your childhood traumas, relationship status, dietary restrictions, or general inner life — all of this is safe from jereme.xyz, which is doing its best to just show you some information about a person and let you contact him if you want to.
§ 5

Third-Party Services and Where Your Tab Goes Next

§ 5.1 — Third-Party Infrastructure in Use

The Site uses the following third-party services. Each has its own privacy documentation that The Operator recommends reviewing if you are the kind of person who reads privacy policies for recreation, in which case: you are already here, so hello and welcome, and also please consider a hobby with more natural light.

  • Cloudflare — DNS, CDN, edge compute, Workers, DDoS protection. The internet's most competent bouncer.
  • Google Analytics (GA4) — anonymous traffic measurement per § 3.2. The Operator uses it to confirm the site exists to people other than himself.

§ 5.2 — Outbound Links Generally

When You click a link on The Site and leave, You are no longer on this site and this Privacy Policy no longer applies to your experience. The destination site has its own rules. Those rules may be more invasive than these rules, which — given that these rules are "he has Google Analytics and a contact form" — is a low bar that most destinations will easily clear. Please read destination privacy policies accordingly or, alternatively, accept that the internet collects data and decide how much that bothers you on a personal level.

§ 5.3 — Social Media Platforms

Links to The Operator's personal social media profiles appear on The Site. These platforms — X, Instagram, Threads, YouTube, Substack, Spotify, GitHub — each have their own data collection practices, and those practices are not described here because describing them would require a separate document that is longer than anything The Operator is willing to produce for a personal website. The platforms collect data when you interact with them. This begins when you arrive on their platforms, not before. The Operator has no control over what they do and is not responsible for it, which is a strange thing to have to say about companies worth more than most countries.

§ 6

Cookies, Technically Speaking

§ 6.1 — The Cookie Situation

There is no aggressive cookie banner on this site. You will not be confronted with a modal asking you to manage your preferences, accept all, decline all, or choose between seventeen categories of tracking technology with ominous names. This is a personal website. The cookie infrastructure is minimal. The absence of a consent banner is not negligence. It reflects the actual absence of the kind of tracking apparatus that requires one.

§ 6.2 — Local Storage

Your theme preference (dark or light mode) is stored in your browser's local storage. This is technically not a cookie. It is mentioned here because the "cookies" section is where people expect to find this information, and The Operator is committed to meeting people where they are. The data lives in your browser. It never leaves. It is, objectively, the most harmless data structure on this entire page.

§ 6.3 — Analytics Cookies

Google Analytics sets cookies to measure aggregate traffic as described in § 3.2. These are measurement cookies, not advertising cookies. They tell The Operator how many people visited, not who they are or where they are going next. If you would like to block them, you may do so at the browser level using any number of freely available extensions. The site will continue to function normally. The Operator will simply have slightly less aggregate data, which is fine, because he was going to do the same thing with it either way: glance at it occasionally and feel vaguely good about the numbers.

§ 6.4 — Third-Party Cookies

Third-party services embedded in The Site — particularly the Spotify widget — may set their own cookies when active. These are governed by those services' own cookie policies. The Operator has limited control over this and is not going to pretend otherwise. If cookie minimalism is important to you, a browser extension like uBlock Origin will handle it. The Operator uses one himself, which should tell you something about his personal position on the matter.

§ 7

Your Rights, Which Are Real and Exercisable

If you are covered by GDPR, CCPA, or any other data protection framework that grants you rights over your personal data, those rights are acknowledged here in full and with genuine respect for the regulatory regimes that produced them, even the ones that generated ninety-page compliance documents that nobody actually reads.

§ 7.1 — Right of Access

You have the right to know what personal data The Operator holds about you. You may request this at any time. The answer is almost certainly: your email address and a message you sent, possibly with a name attached. The Operator will confirm this in writing within a timeframe that is "reasonable" in the plain English sense of the word, not the legal sense, which tends toward ninety days for reasons that have never been satisfactorily explained.

§ 7.2 — Right to Erasure

You have the right to request deletion of any personal data The Operator holds. This request will be honored without argument, without requiring you to justify it, and without invoking retention schedules or legitimate interest exceptions, because this is a personal website and none of that applies. Email and ask. It will be deleted. That is the entire process.

§ 7.3 — Right to Object and Correct

You have the right to object to processing of your data or request correction of inaccurate data. Given that the only personal data The Operator holds is what you submitted, the most likely inaccuracy is a typo in your own email address, which you can simply resend correctly. The right exists. It is yours. Exercising it on a personal website contact form response is one of the more low-stakes instances of GDPR compliance in recorded history, but the right is no less real for that.

§ 7.4 — How to Exercise Any of These Rights

Email The Operator. The contact form is on the homepage. State what you want. He will do it. There is no ticket system. There is no case number. There is no portal to log into. There is a human being who built the site, operates the site, and manages all data inquiries personally. It is, in some respects, the most direct data rights process on the internet.

§ 10

Updates, Amendments, and The Notification System

§ 10.1 — Right to Update

The Operator may update this Privacy Policy at any time for any reason — regulatory changes, new third-party services, new features, corrections, or the discovery that a previous section was less clear than intended. Changes take effect when published. This is how websites have worked since the beginning of websites. It has never been a satisfying answer to "but when will you tell me" and it remains unsatisfying now.

§ 10.2 — The Notification System, Described Honestly

When this policy is updated, the document metadata at the top of this page will change. That is it. That is the notification system. The Operator will not email you because he almost certainly does not have your email address. He will not post about it on social media because it would be a deeply boring post. If this policy changes in a way that materially affects you and you would like to know about it, you may bookmark this page and check it occasionally. That is the entire available mechanism. The Operator is sorry. The GDPR would like a word with the general convention of how websites handle this and so would he.

§ 10.3 — Continued Use as Acceptance

Continued use of The Site after any update constitutes acceptance of the updated policy. If you find this unacceptable, you may stop visiting. The Operator genuinely hopes you don't, because traffic makes the analytics dashboard look better, but he respects the decision. He has made his peace with it. He will survive.
