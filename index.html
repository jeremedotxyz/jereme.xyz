Evaluation & Feedback

Design & Product // nrthrn

Function is the brief. Everything else is craft.

Tech and industrial goods designed for utility, refined by human judgment. Prototypes to production. Consumer hardware to tooling. Nothing gratuitous, nothing wasted. Objects built to work exactly the way they look.

Industrial Design

Form follows function, then gets refined until there's nothing left to remove. Consumer goods, tools, and hardware designed for the way people actually use things.

Prototyping

From concept to physical object. Iterative builds, material selection, tolerance testing. The gap between a good idea and a manufacturable product is where most things die.

Hardware Development

Consumer electronics, embedded systems, peripheral devices. Hardware that ships to millions is a different discipline than hardware that ships to one client. Both matter here.
Hardware to Market

18 years from concept to shipping product.

Former PM on hardware at a large tech company — camera systems, audio hardware, smart displays at consumer scale. That experience lives in every nrthrn project. Understanding supply chain, manufacturing tolerances, and what "done" actually means when a product ships to millions.

Independent builds operate differently. Smaller runs, tighter margins, more direct material decisions. The discipline is the same. The stakes are different.

Visual Systems

Brand architecture built to last, not to launch.

Consumer and industrial objects designed from the material out. Every decision justified by use, every surface earned. The gap between a thing that works and a thing that feels inevitable is where the actual work lives.

The scale ranges from a single prototype to full production runs. The clients range from manufacturers to independent operators building something that didn't exist before. The object doesn't care who made it — it either does what it's supposed to do, or it doesn't.

Physical–Digital

Objects and interfaces share the same rules.

Industrial and UI design both reduce to the same question: what does the person need to happen, and what gets in the way. The medium changes — aluminum, glass, CSS, firmware — the discipline doesn't.

nrthrn sits at that intersection deliberately. Consumer hardware, digital interfaces, tooling, and physical goods under one roof. Cross-pollination is the point.
